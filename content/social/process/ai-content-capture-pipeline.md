## Twitter thread (9 tweets)

**Tweet 1 — hook**

Every conversation I have already becomes a draft. I just built the pipe that catches it before it disappears.

---

**Tweet 2 — capture**

Step one is capture, not writing.

Meetings get recorded by an app called Granola. Talking out loud at home gets picked up by a dictation app called Whisper Flow.

---

**Tweet 3 — the journal**

Both feed one running journal. Raw, unedited, just me spitballing. Nothing gets lost because nothing gets cleaned up yet.

---

**Tweet 4 — polish**

Next comes polish: one pass that reshapes the raw journal into an actual idea, sharper language, a real throughline instead of scattered thoughts. I can steer it too: tell it to build a story around one specific memory instead of just summarizing everything I said.

---

**Tweet 5 — syndicate**

From there it sorts into one of four topics I write about: process, experiment, breakdown, authority. Each becomes a blog post, then tweets and a LinkedIn post shaped for that topic.

---

**Tweet 6 — the motivation hack**

I pay for the scheduling tool that queues my posts, even during the free trial.

Money on the line is the only accountability that's ever actually worked on me.

---

**Tweet 7 — the number**

158 posts scheduled out to September, right now.

---

**Tweet 8 — proof it's not just for tweets**

Same pipeline, different business: I fed my dad's sales meetings into it and got back a call list and a script for his team, right after we cut the ad spend that wasn't converting.

---

**Tweet 9 — kicker**

This thread went through that exact pipeline: journal, polish, syndicate. If you already talk about your work, you already have the material. Build the system that catches it.

## LinkedIn post (~220 words)

Every conversation I have already becomes a draft. I built a pipeline that catches it before it disappears.

Meetings get recorded through an app called Granola. Talking at home gets picked up by a dictation app called Whisper Flow. Neither one asks me to write anything. That's on purpose.

Both feed a running journal, raw and unedited. From there, one pass called polish reshapes it into an actual idea. I can steer that pass too: tell it to build a story around one memory instead of summarizing everything I said. The idea then sorts into one of four topics I write about and becomes a blog post, a set of tweets, and a LinkedIn post.

Finished posts land in a backlog, then get queued in a scheduling tool I pay for on purpose, even during the free trial. Money on the line is the only accountability that's ever actually worked on me. Right now that backlog holds 158 posts scheduled out to September.

I ran the same pipeline on my dad's sales meetings and got back a call list and a script for his team, after we cut the ad spend that wasn't converting. Same system, different business.

This post went through that exact pipeline. If you already talk about your work, you already have the material. The system is what's usually missing.
