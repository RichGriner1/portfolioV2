# AI workflow — how I actually work with AI

Standalone-only, Matt Gray frameworks (see `content-os/05-content-production/tweet-frameworks.md`). Source tag: **AIWF**. Grounded in Richard's real practice — Wispr Flow voice notes, Granola meeting transcripts, Claude Code builds, framework porting.

Substance gate: process posts aren't exempt from the value bar. Each of these leads with the *transferable* move (the principle), not a workflow tour. The tool is incidental; the takeaway travels.

Skeleton legend: §4 you-don't-need-go-find · §9 stop-X-do-Y · §10 contrarian-lever · §11 setup-payoff-kicker · §17 one-sentence opener.

**Dual-use flag:** posts marked ✅ LI double as short LinkedIn posts (same copy, more line spacing). This is the Matt-Gray move — a sharp standalone works on both platforms without a separate 200-word rewrite.

---

**Standalone A — think in the park, build at the desk** *(§17)* — ✅ LI

I do my best design thinking in a park, not at my desk.

I walk through Casa de Campo talking into Wispr Flow — the project, what I'm actually trying to build, why it matters. Then I come home and hand the transcript to Claude Code. The thinking's done by the time I sit down. The desk is just where I build it.

---

**Standalone B — spoken reasoning beats typed prompts** *(§10)* — ✅ LI

The most useful thing I feed an AI isn't a prompt I wrote for it.

It's a Granola transcript of me talking through a decision in a meeting — before I knew I'd reuse it. Type at an AI and you self-edit. Talk through a problem and you say the real reasons. The unedited version is the one worth keeping.

---

**Standalone C — extract your voice, don't write it** *(§9 / §10)* — ✅ LI

I didn't write my voice guide. I extracted it.

Fed a stack of my transcripts and old posts to an AI and asked what stays consistent — sentence length, the phrases I lean on, where I hedge and where I don't. Rules pulled from how I already write, not invented. You know your voice by feel; the pattern-matcher can name it.

---

**Standalone D — mine your folders, don't brainstorm** *(§4)* — ✅ LI

You don't need to brainstorm content.

Almost everything I post comes out of project folders and meeting transcripts I already have — the real number, the decision that got reversed, the bug that turned into a rule. Mining what happened beats inventing what sounds good. The specifics are already on your drive.

---

**Standalone E — get the AI to interview you** *(§9)* — ✅ LI

Stop asking AI to write your post. Ask it to interview you.

Get interviewed about a project — why that call, what broke, what you'd change — and you say things you didn't know you thought. The draft comes out of the answers. The questions do the work a blank page can't.

---

**Standalone F — the framework stopped being the wall** *(§11)*

What used to take me forever wasn't the design. It was rebuilding it in the project's framework.

Now I take a working implementation and have AI port it to Angular to match the stack — React projects stay React. The pattern travels; the framework stops being the wall between "designed" and "shipped."
