# Tweet Frameworks

Reusable structural skeletons for X posts (and short LinkedIn). Distilled from Matt Gray's "Tweet Templates" vault (sourced 2026-06 from Richard's Notion; the vault itself is dated ~July 2022).

> **Read this first — the guardrail.** These are *structures*, not voice. Matt Gray's originals are founder-guru maxims ("___ is a drug," "___ is the ultimate motivation") — most of which would **fail Richard's voice guide**. Below are only the skeletons that fit a **story-first practitioner**, each rewritten into Richard's design/AI/fintech world. Every one must start from a **real, specific moment** — never a generic maxim. If a framework can't be filled with a true lived detail, don't use it.
>
> **Dated-source note:** the vault says "publish threads 2x/week." That was 2022. Matt's 2026 feed (see `02-voice-guide/examples/liked-posts.md`) is all standalone. Default to standalone; these skeletons are mostly single posts.

## The 6 that fit Richard

### 1. The biggest-mistake observation
**Skeleton:** The biggest mistake I made as {role}: {mistake}. {one line of context}. Now I: — {action 1} — {action 2} — {action 3}. {what changed}.
**Richard example:**
> The biggest mistake I made as a design system designer: I named a color "AFI azul."
> The day we onboarded a second client, the whole system broke.
> Now I name by function — `color-surface-secondary`.
> Brand names don't scale.

### 2. Lessons from doing the thing
**Skeleton:** {Did this real thing}. {short context}. Here are {N} lessons I learned in {time}: 1… 2… 3… {honest sign-off}.
**Richard example:**
> Shipped a fintech simulator as the only non-technical person in the room.
> 5 things it taught me about design surviving the build: 1…

### 3. Old way → new way
**Skeleton:** How to {do the thing}: Old way: {a, b, c}. New way: {a, b, c}. {New} > {Old}.
**Richard example:**
> How I hand off design now:
> Old way: static Figma, devs guess the states.
> New way: tokens matched to code, states defined, the grid is real.
> Defined > implied.

### 4. You don't need X, go find Y
**Skeleton:** You don't need {the thing people chase}. Go find: — {real thing 1} — {real thing 2} — {real thing 3}. {why}. {conclusion}.
**Richard example:**
> You don't need a 200-page design system.
> Go find: your 10 real components, your token layers, your states.
> Most systems die from over-documentation, not under.

### 5. It's simple — don't need / do need
**Skeleton:** {Topic} is simple. Things you don't need: {x, y, z}. Things you do need: {a, b, c}.
**Richard example:**
> A design.md is simple.
> Don't need: every rule written down, a 50-page doc.
> Do need: tokens, component structure, and the 3 conventions you keep breaking.

### 6. The N things that drive the outcome (list-post + kicker)
**Skeleton:** The {N} {things} that {outcome}: — {1} — {2} — {3}. {punchy kicker line}.
**Richard example:**
> 3 things I check before any handoff:
> — token layers match code
> — states are defined
> — the grid is real
> Miss one and the devs design it for you.

## More single-tweet skeletons (added 2026-07, from a fresh Matt Gray capture)

Same rule: fill each with a **real, specific moment**, never a maxim. Reframes must be *earned* (see `02-voice-guide/voice-guide.md` → "Reframes — earned vs AI-y").

### 7. The equation
**Skeleton:** {Outcome} = {A} + {B} + {C}.
**Matt:** "Great Hire = High Intelligence + High Energy + High Integrity"
**Richard example:**
> Clean handoff = tokens that match code + defined states + a real grid.

### 8. The comparison
**Skeleton:** {X} > {Y}. Ultra-short — one belief stated as a ranking.
**Matt:** "Systems > Goals"
**Richard example:**
> Consistency > flexibility. A template every page shares beats a bespoke layout per screen.

### 9. Stop X. Do Y.
**Skeleton:** Stop {the common move}. {The better move instead}.
**Matt:** "Stop searching for motivation. Build systems that don't require it."
**Richard example:**
> Stop handing engineers static frames. Hand them code.

### 10. The contrarian lever
**Skeleton:** {Result / credential}. The lever wasn't {the obvious thing}. It was {the real thing}.
**Matt:** "I built a $15M business with a team of 12. The lever wasn't headcount. It was the content engine, the AI systems, and a calendar that stays mostly empty."
**Richard example:**
> We got AI to generate on-brand UI. The lever wasn't a better prompt. It was naming our tokens by function.

*(Uses the "wasn't X, it was Y" reveal — allowed because it's earned and specific here. Keep it rare; hollow, it's the #1 AI-tell.)*

### 11. Setup → payoff → kicker
**Skeleton:** {The small sacrifice}. {What it buys}. {The one-line lesson}.
**Matt:** "Saying no to 100 hires. To land 1 A-player. That's the math of a lean empire."
**Richard example:**
> Deleting 35 lines of CSS from three pages. To gain one shared template. Now every new page starts consistent.

## From the full Notion vault (2026-07 deep pull)

Pulled the whole Matt Gray "Tweet Templates" Notion (Educate, Observations, Challenge Me, One-Sentence Openers; thread categories skipped). Most of it was already captured above or is guru-maxim filler. These are the additional *structures* worth keeping — each rewritten into a real design/AI/fintech moment. Frames that fail the voice guide are listed at the bottom.

### 12. Simple advice (imperative list, no kicker)
**Skeleton:** Simple advice that will change {how you do X}: — {imperative} — {imperative} — {imperative}
**Richard example:**
> Simple advice that will change your design handoff:
> — Name tokens by function, not color
> — Define every state, not just the happy path
> — Put the rules in a file the AI can read
> — Audit the AI's first pass against your variables

### 13. Every {role} should understand
**Skeleton:** Every {specific role} should understand: — {thing} — {thing} — {thing}
**Richard example:**
> Every designer shipping with AI should understand:
> — tokens travel; screens don't
> — your primitive → semantic → component tiers
> — why "primary" is a name an AI guesses wrong
> — how to read the code back

### 14. The breakdown (percentages, with a surprise)
**Skeleton:** {Thing} is {X}% {expected}, {Y}% {expected}, and {Z}% {the unexpected real thing}. The surprise carries the tweet.
**Richard example:**
> A design system is 20% components, 30% naming, and 50% getting people to actually use it.

### 15. Stages ladder
**Skeleton:** Stages of {topic}: Beginner: … / Intermediate: … / Advanced: … / Master: …
**Richard example:**
> Stages of a design system:
> Beginner: you name the colors.
> Intermediate: you name the roles.
> Advanced: you stop naming and let the tokens decide.
> Master: the AI reads them and you barely touch it.

### 16. 99% skip this
**Skeleton:** 99% of {X} skip {the one thing}: {short list}. Too much {the easy thing}. Not enough {the real thing}.
**Richard example:**
> 99% of design handoffs skip the states.
> Hover, focus, disabled, empty, loading.
> Too much polish on the happy path. Not enough on the other five.

### 17. One-sentence opener (hook seed)
**Skeleton:** A single sharp line to open a post; the real point follows. Frames: "{X} is not the hard part. {Y} is." · "{X} is the whole game." · "{X} is you against {force}."
**Richard examples:**
> Naming is not the boring part. It's the load-bearing part.
> Consistency is the whole game.
> A design system is you against entropy.

**⛔ Skip from the vault (fails the voice guide):**
- **Empty-maxim frames:** "Nothing is more attractive than ___", "___ is an underrated vibe", "Your ability to ___ determines your ___ level", "Where there is ___ there is growth", "___ kills ___". Guru-bait with no lived backing — only touch one if a *real, specific* moment fills it completely.
- **The Engagement category** and **"Do X every day — people will either react A / B / C."** That's engagement-bait. Reshape into a genuine question from a real moment, or skip.
- **The guru CTA stack** ("Whenever you're ready, there are 4 ways I can help you…") — already banned. Richard has a full-time job.

## Transitions (Seeds of Curiosity)

For the rare thread, or a longer LinkedIn post — short lines that pull the reader down. Use sparingly; Richard already does this naturally ("But it wasn't scalable.").

- "But it wasn't scalable." · "Then it hit me." · "Here's why." · "Let me explain." · "But there's a catch." · "And this is what happened."

---

## ⛔ Avoid (fails the voice guide)

- **Empty maxims:** "___ is a drug," "___ is the ultimate motivation," "___ is the biggest life hack," "Conquering ___ will let you build your dream life." Generic, guru-bait, AI-tell. Banned.
- **Aphorisms with no lived backing.** A one-liner is only allowed if it compresses a *real* moment (see voice-guide.md → "earned, not preached").
- **The "Whenever you're ready, 4 ways I can help you" CTA stack.** That's a sales funnel. Richard has a full-time job — never.
- **Manufactured curiosity** ("You won't believe what happened next") when nothing actually did.

---

## How to use this

During drafting (`content-drafting-prompt.md` / `standalone-tweets-template.md`): pick a skeleton that matches the story's shape, then fill it with verbatim Granola details. If you're reaching for a skeleton because the post feels empty, that's the signal the *story* is weak — go back to the Story Bank, don't force a template.
