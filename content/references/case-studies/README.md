# Case-study references

Six case studies from two Madrid studios, broken down so we can borrow their structure when we write ours. This folder is about **content and structure**: which sections a case study has, what each one says, and which graphic sits next to it. Motion references get their own folder later (`content/references/motion/`).

The `case-study` skill points here. Read this folder before drafting a new case study or restructuring an old one.

## What these pages do well, in one list

1. **Proof before story.** The Cocktail puts a line of results (`+40% leads ··· −63% campaign costs`) directly under the title. You know the outcome before you read the problem.
2. **The title is a transformation.** "From a landing page to a new venture." "From one-off provider to strategic technology partner." Before and after, in one line.
3. **A fixed metadata block.** Client, where, what, when (Cocktail). Industry, services, timeline (Garaje). Services doubles as the capability list.
4. **Numbered beats.** 01 The challenge, 02 The solution, Conclusion. The reader always knows where they are.
5. **Every graphic answers the sentence next to it.** "Multiple platforms and technologies" sits beside a bubble diagram of Angular, Ionic, middleware and backend. "Omnichannel" sits beside a watch and a TV. Nothing is decoration.
6. **A human voice mid-page.** One client quote, placed after the solution and before the results.
7. **Time as structure.** A dated timeline (2018 → Feb 2019 → Mar 2019 → Jun 2019) or giant years (2021 → 202x) shows the work took real time.
8. **Results close the page, as numbers with captions.** Three big figures, each with one line saying what it measures.

## Where they fall short, and where we need more

They sell a studio to a buyer. We sell one designer to a hiring team, so we need things they skip:

- **Who did what.** Agency pages say "we". A hiring manager needs Richard's role, team size and the decisions he owned.
- **Decisions and trade-offs.** None of the six shows an option that was dropped. Our `StoryOption` rows (`proposed / chosen / dropped`) cover this; use them.
- **The before state.** Banco Azteca never shows the old ATM. Solar360 never shows the landing page it started from. A before screen or a diagram of the old mess makes the after credible.
- **A miss.** Our brand guide asks for one admitted miss or trade-off per case study. These pages never admit one.
- **Numbers, consistently.** IKEA, Iberdrola and CaixaBank Now have no metrics at all. Garaje leans on team growth and scope instead. When we have no outcome numbers, count the system (components, tokens, screens, products on it).
- **Information architecture and flows.** Only Adeslas draws a diagram. The rest jump from photo to finished screen. Our work is systems work, so the diagram is often the most honest graphic we have.
