# Research Brief Template

A research brief is created **only after a story is chosen** and gap analysis has run. Research exists to *support* lived experience — to add evidence, context, a number, or a counterpoint. It never becomes the spine of the piece.

**Rule of thumb:** if the research is doing more than supporting your story, you've inverted the system. Stop and re-center on the story.

---

## Template (copy this block)

```markdown
# Research Brief: [story title / ID]

- **Story this supports:** [Story Bank ID]
- **Date created:**
- **Status:** open | in-progress | complete

## The story (one paragraph)
[Restate the lived experience this research supports, so the research never drifts from it.]

## What I'm claiming
[The opinion / point the story makes. Research must support THIS, not replace it.]

## Research questions (only the gaps)
Only list things that genuinely need outside evidence. Lived-experience gaps go to the interview, not here.

1.
2.
3.

## Constraints
- Keep it supporting, not central.
- Prefer primary sources and real examples over think-pieces.
- Flag anything that contradicts the story (don't hide it — it might make the piece stronger).

## Findings
| # | Finding | Source (link) | How it supports the story | Confidence |
|---|---------|---------------|---------------------------|------------|
| 1 |         |               |                           | high/med/low |

## Contradictions / tension found
[Anything that pushes back on the claim. This is valuable — it can sharpen the argument or become the piece's honest counterpoint.]

## Verdict
- Does the research strengthen the story? [yes / no / partially]
- What to actually USE in the draft (and what to leave out).
- Anything still missing → back to interview or capture.
```

---

## Example (abbreviated)

```markdown
# Research Brief: AI changes the feedback dynamic (ai-feedback-dynamic-2026-06-18)

## What I'm claiming
Documenting design reasoning with AI *before* review changes what feedback is about — from taste to logic.

## Research questions (only the gaps)
1. Is there published work on "design rationale" / decision records that I can connect my experience to? (To place my lived take in context — not to argue for me.)
2. Any data on revision-cycle reduction when rationale is documented up front?

## Findings
| # | Finding | Source | How it supports | Confidence |
|---|---------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| 1 | "Architecture Decision Records" are an established practice in eng | (link) | Lets me say "design is borrowing what eng already learned" | high |

## Verdict
Use finding #1 as a single supporting line. The story stays the spine. Don't over-cite.
```
