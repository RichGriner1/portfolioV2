## LinkedIn post (~195 words)

The most time-consuming part of my job used to be checking that a number on a table matched the same number on the graph next to it. That was never the valuable part.

One financial-planning flow we built ran close to ten thousand euros for the client. A real slice of that money paid for me manually re-checking numbers, not for a single design decision. Get one wrong and the client would be furious, so the checking got done slowly, by hand, every time.

None of that made the product better. It just kept it from being worse.

AI generating the first pass of a screen took that verification work off my plate. Now that time goes to the decisions that actually matter: how a flow should work, what a client needs to see first, what the interface is even for.

I hope that's what the job keeps becoming: designing the experience, not confirming that a chart agrees with a table.

The work that eats the most hours isn't always the work that's worth the most. Removing the first kind is what makes room for the second.
