## LinkedIn post (~215 words)

"What should this look like?" is too big a question to answer in one sitting. So I stopped trying.

Working through our product's interface, I went piece by piece: what should the buttons look like? The menus? The cards, the sliders, the tabs? Each one got its own small stack of reference screenshots, pulled from Mobin, a site that collects screens from real, shipped apps.

The answers converged fast. The same handful of apps, Shopify, Clerk, Notion, kept showing up across pieces of the interface that had nothing to do with each other.

The harder part came next: turning fifteen small decisions into one coherent design system, not fifteen unrelated screens. Piecemeal requests to an AI don't do that. Ask for a button, get a button. Ask for a menu next, and it won't match. What works is putting everything, the notes, the screenshots, the reasoning, into one folder and handing an AI that whole folder in a single prompt.

We haven't run that prompt yet. That's next week's session.

One big vague question is where good taste gets stuck. Small, answerable ones are how you get unstuck, one piece at a time.
