## Twitter thread (5 tweets)

**Tweet 1 — hook**

Asked an AI to write color rules for one icon: normal, hover, pressed, disabled, selected. Five situations.

It handed back four.

---

**Tweet 2 — the mechanism**

No warning that one was missing. No flag. The answer looked finished: four clean rules standing in for five.

We'd loaded it up: colors, spacing, and type rules, all dumped into one request. One condition, for one icon, got lost in that pile.

---

**Tweet 3 — the insight**

An AI doesn't raise its hand when it's overwhelmed. It just quietly leaves something out.

---

**Tweet 4 — the fix**

The fix wasn't a smarter prompt. It was a smaller one.

One category of decisions at a time: colors, then spacing, then type. Check each before moving to the next.

---

**Tweet 5 — kicker**

The same rule applies outside design tools. Think of a new hire handed five tasks at once: same pattern, one gets dropped, not from carelessness, but from nobody being able to hold five threads and flag the gap. Chunk the work instead.

## LinkedIn post (~160 words)

We asked an AI to write the color rules for one icon: what it looks like normal, on hover, when pressed, disabled, and selected. Five clear situations.

It came back with four. No warning, no flag, nothing that said it had skipped one. The answer just looked finished.

Looks like we'd loaded it up: colors, spacing (the gaps between elements), and type (fonts) rules, all dumped into one request. Somewhere in that pile, one condition for one icon quietly went missing, and it stayed missing until someone counted the list against what we'd asked for.

The fix wasn't a smarter prompt. It was a smaller one. We started working through one category at a time: colors, then spacing, then type, checking each before moving to the next.

An AI doesn't raise its hand when it's overwhelmed. Neither does a first draft, or a new hire handed five things at once. Whatever you don't check for gets skipped, and you find out later.
