## LinkedIn post (~210 words)

A coworker read a stack of my drafted tweets in one sitting and told me something I didn't expect: he could tell they were AI-written.

Not because any single tweet was bad. Each one read clean on its own, no rough edges, nothing that felt unfinished. The problem only showed up once he read several in a row. They all had the same rhythm and the same finish. That sameness is what gave it away, not any one sentence.

He added a fair caveat: most readers won't catch this. It mainly reads to people who already use AI daily and recognize its habits. I'd bet that group keeps growing, and eventually it includes whoever you're actually trying to reach.

The fix wasn't a smarter instruction. It was better reference material. Instead of asking the AI to write from a blank page, I feed it real paragraphs from Matt Gray's writing before asking for a draft. It has to imitate a specific voice instead of falling back on its own default one.

If you're using AI for your marketing, the same tell is probably showing up in your posts. Not in any one of them. Across all of them. Show the model a voice instead of asking it for one.
