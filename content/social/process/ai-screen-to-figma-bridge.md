## LinkedIn post (~200 words)

I used to spend the first hour of any new design staring at a blank screen. I don't anymore.

I ask an AI, I use Claude, to generate a rough version of the screen first, as a simple web page. A browser add-on then pulls that page into Figma, the tool I actually design in, so it lands as something I can click into and edit, not just a picture of a screen I'd have to rebuild from scratch.

The AI's draft is never good enough to ship. That's not the point. The point is that "change this" is a faster conversation to have with a client than "imagine something and tell me if you'd like it." A rough version gives them something to react to. Nothing gives them nothing to say.

I use this before any of the real design work starts, the reusable pieces meant to last. It buys client feedback cheap, before the expensive part begins.

One honest note: I've used up the free download limit on every email account I own doing this. Worth paying for at this point.

The fastest way through a blank page isn't a better first idea. It's any idea you can point at and correct.
