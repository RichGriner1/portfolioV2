# Research Workflow

How research moves through Content OS. The single most important thing to understand: **research comes after the story, and only supports it.** This workflow exists so research never takes over.

---

## Where research sits in the weekly loop

```
Story Bank  →  Weekly prioritization  →  [STORY CHOSEN]
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                     Gap analysis (what's missing?)
                                              │
                        ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
                        ▼                                            ▼
              "Ask Richard" gaps                          "Go research" gaps
              → interview questions                       → research brief (this folder)
                        │                                            │
                        └─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                              ▼
                              Drafting (story + research + interview + voice)
```

Research only handles the gaps that genuinely need *outside* evidence. Anything that lives in your own head or experience goes to the interview, not to research.

---

## The steps

1. **Don't start until a story is chosen.** No story, no research. Full stop.
2. **Run gap analysis** (`gap-analysis-prompt.md`) on the chosen story. It splits gaps into:
   - **Lived gaps** → handled by interview questions (`04-interview/`).
   - **Evidence gaps** → handled here, by a research brief.
3. **Write a research brief** (`research-brief-template.md`) for the evidence gaps only. Keep the list short — each item must earn its place.
4. **Gather supporting evidence.** Prefer primary sources, real examples, and concrete numbers over opinion pieces. Cite everything with links.
5. **Capture contradictions.** If research pushes back on the story, write it down. Don't bury it — a real counterpoint often makes the piece stronger and more honest.
6. **Decide what to actually use.** Most research should *not* make it into the draft. Pull only the one or two findings that genuinely strengthen the story. The rest stays in the brief as backing.
7. **Hand off to drafting.** The draft uses the story as the spine and the research as occasional support.

---

## Guardrails (read before every research session)

- **The story is the spine. Research is a buttress.** If you're writing *around* research instead of *around* a story, stop.
- **Don't research to find a story.** That produces generic content. Research validates and supports what you already lived.
- **Less is more.** One sharp, well-sourced fact beats five. Over-citing reads as defensive and academic.
- **Cite real sources.** No vague "studies show." Link it or cut it.
- **Surface tension, don't smooth it.** Contradictions are material, not problems.
- **Keep Richard's vocabulary.** Research can inform, but the language stays his.

---

## When research reveals there's no story

Sometimes gap analysis or research shows the "story" is really just a topic you have an opinion about but haven't *lived*. When that happens:

- Don't draft. Generic content starts exactly here.
- Move the idea back to `06-content-ideas/content-ideas.md` and flag it: "needs a real story / lived example before producing."
- Ask: is there an actual experience I can connect this to? If yes, capture it in the Story Bank first.
