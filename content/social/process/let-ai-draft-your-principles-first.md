---

**Tweet 2 — the gate**

The rule going in: nothing gets built until the foundation is settled. What colors mean, how much space is enough, what style we're deliberately not copying. All decided before anyone opens a layout, the actual screens people will use.

---

**Tweet 3 — what we wrote ourselves**

One principle we wrote ourselves before handing the rest to the AI: make every graph interactive, something a user can actually play with, not a static chart. It was the one request we kept hearing from the team.

**Tweet 4 — the correction**

The AI's list included "consistent" as a principle. We cut it. Too vague to settle an argument later, since anyone could point to it and mean something different. A real principle has to be specific enough to end a disagreement, not just sound nice.

**Tweet 5 — kicker**

The AI's draft ended up better than a blank page would have gotten us alone, not because it's smarter, because correcting a draft beats starting one. Write your rules first, whoever drafts them, before you build anything.

## LinkedIn post (~190 words)

We let an AI write our design principles before we built a single screen. It did a noticeably better job than we would have on our own.

The rule going in: nothing gets built until the foundation is settled. Principles first, meaning the rules that guide every design decision on the project. What colors are allowed to mean. How much space is enough. What we're deliberately not going to copy from other products.

Before handing the rest to the AI, I wrote one principle myself: every graph should be something a user can play with, not a flat picture. It was the one thing the team kept asking for.

The AI wrote the rest, and its list beat what we'd have written alone starting from nothing. One line still needed a correction: it had listed "consistent" as a principle. We cut it. Too vague to settle a real argument, since two people could point to the same word and mean opposite things.

A rulebook you correct is faster than a rulebook you start from a blank page. Write yours down before you build anything, whoever drafts the first version.
