# Showing the work: visuals for case studies

How a case-study visual should look. Collected 2026-11-27 from inspora.design (designers posting their own work) and from what went wrong on the Wealth Planner case study. Images in [visuals/](visuals/).

## The rule

**Compose, don't crop.** A case-study visual is a composition made for its slot: one or two real components, floating on a quiet background, scaled as a whole. It is never a crop of a screen and never a screen recording shrunk down. The live demo is where the whole screen lives.

## What the good examples do

| Example | What it shows | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| [inspora-grid.webp](visuals/inspora-grid.webp) | A feed of designers' own posts | Every tile is a composition with a lot of air. Nothing touches the edge. |
| [inspora-reader-card.png](visuals/inspora-reader-card.png) | One reading card with its toolbar | One component, centred, soft shadow, text fading at the top and bottom instead of being cut. |

## Checklist for every visual

1. **One idea.** Say in a sentence what the visual proves. If it needs two sentences, it's two visuals or a selector.
2. **Whole components.** Nothing cut by the frame. If an element is too wide, compose a narrower version of it, don't crop it.
3. **Scale the composition, not the crop.** The composition has a fixed shape (16:10 by default) and scales as a whole, so a phone sees the same picture, smaller.
4. **Real components.** Built from the product's design system, not redrawn. Redraws look lo-fi next to the real thing.
5. **Quiet stage.** A light, near-flat background, soft shadows, air on every side. The product carries the colour.
6. **Annotate when the point is "where".** Numbered markers on the screen beat a paragraph that lists places.
7. **Before is the shipped version.** Label it with what it was ("the live product"), not an internal version number.
