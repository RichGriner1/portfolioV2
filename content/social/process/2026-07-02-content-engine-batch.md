# Content engine — building the system that makes the content

Standalone-only. Source tag: **CENG**. The meta story: how this whole content system got built — capturing raw material (Granola meetings, AI chats, Wispr Flow voice notes), writing with examples instead of instructions, and the human-in-the-loop edit ritual that keeps AI output from drifting into slop. Every one is `✅ LI` dual-use.

Skeleton legend: §10 contrarian-lever · §9 stop/do · §17 one-sentence opener.

**Substance gate:** each leads with the transferable move (examples > instructions, the edit loop, taste as bottleneck), not a tool tour.

---

**Standalone A — examples beat instructions** *(§10)* — ✅ LI

The biggest jump in my AI writing didn't come from a better prompt.

It came from examples. I gave it my old posts and a few writers I actually admire, and the output went from generic to mine. Describe your style to an AI and you get a description back. Show it, and you get the thing.

---

**Standalone B — cross-reference to find the real topic** *(§9)* — ✅ LI

I stopped brainstorming what to post.

Instead I cross-reference three things I already have: meeting transcripts, AI chats, and voice notes from walks. When the same frustration shows up in all three, that's the post. Real patterns beat clever ideas.

---

**Standalone C — the edit is the work now** *(§17)* — ✅ LI

AI writes a week of drafts in an hour. A third of them don't survive a second read.

That's not a failure — it's the point. The work moved from writing to editing. The generation is free; the judgment isn't.

---

**Standalone D — the loop that keeps it good** *(§17)* — ✅ LI

How I keep AI content from drifting into slop:

Generate all week. Edit one day a week. Every correction I make gets written back into the style guide, so the same mistake doesn't return. The AI improves because I teach it — not because it guessed better next time.

---

**Standalone E — taste is the bottleneck** *(§10)* — ✅ LI

AI didn't take the writing off my plate. It moved the work from typing to deciding.

Drafting used to be the slow part. Now it's free, and the slow part is knowing what's actually good. Taste is the piece that doesn't automate.
