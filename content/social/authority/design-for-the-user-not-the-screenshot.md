## LinkedIn post (~190 words)

A financial advisor stares at one screen for eight hours. A screenshot of that same screen gets about ten seconds of anyone else's attention.

The default instinct on most screens is to make them look calm: lots of white space, a light touch, few things fighting for attention. On one enterprise fintech product we're building, that instinct ran the wrong way. The people using it aren't glancing at it, they're working a full day inside it, scanning numbers, comparing rows, moving between panels without wanting to click through to find something.

When we caught ourselves defaulting to "make it look cleaner," we stopped and asked who the screen was actually for. The answer changed the call: density needed to win over visual cleanliness, on purpose, even though cleaning things up is our habit.

Clean is a great look for a screenshot. It's not the same thing as good for the person doing the job inside it.

Design the demo for ten seconds and the product for the eight hours somebody spends there. Those are two different jobs, and only one of them is the one your user is paying for.
