# LinkedIn Post Template

One clear takeaway. A real moment up top. Practical and scannable. Written for designers, founders of smaller businesses, and people figuring out AI in design and product.

**Hard rule:** no "DM me," no freelance pitches. Richard has a full-time job. The post earns attention by being useful, not by selling.

---

## Structure

```markdown
---
pillar:
story_id:
status: draft
target_date:
---

[HOOK — first line. This is the whole game. A real moment or a sharp claim. It must make someone stop scrolling. NOT a question, NOT "I've been thinking about…". Drop them into something concrete.]

[Line break — LinkedIn truncates after ~2 lines, so the first line + this must earn the "see more" click.]

[THE STORY — 2–4 short paragraphs. What happened. Specific: the project, the moment, the number. Short lines. White space between them. Mobile-readable.]

[THE TAKEAWAY — the one thing to remember. State the opinion plainly. The reasoning, compressed.]

[CLOSE — land it. End on the insight, or a genuine question that invites real discussion (not "thoughts? 👇"). One honest question, if any.]

[Optional: 2–4 relevant hashtags, lowercase, specific. Not a wall of tags.]
```

---

## Rules for this template

- **First line is everything.** If it doesn't stop the scroll, the post fails. Test it cold.
- **One takeaway only.** LinkedIn rewards a single clear point, not a kitchen sink.
- **Short lines, lots of white space.** Read it on a phone before shipping.
- **Keep the opinion sharp.** Don't soften to be "professional." A real stance is what gets engagement that matters.
- **No pitch, no DM-me, no humblebrag.** Useful and honest.
- **Use Richard's vocabulary** and verbatim phrases from the transcript.
- Usually derived from a blog or a strong Story Bank entry — the LinkedIn post is the compressed version of one idea.

---

## Quick hook patterns (use real ones, don't force)

- The blunt lesson: "I shipped a product I didn't fully understand technically. Here's what that actually taught me."
- The contrarian claim: "AI didn't make me faster. It changed what my feedback meetings are even about."
- The specific moment: "Three revision cycles on one AFI component. Then I changed one thing and it dropped to zero."
