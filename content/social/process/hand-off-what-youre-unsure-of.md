## LinkedIn post

A design token is a named decision, something like "brand blue," that you set once and reuse everywhere, so every screen points back to the same answer instead of guessing it fresh each time. That's the whole idea. The rest is just where you draw the lines.

I'd already made my own taste calls on this system: the exact colors, the numbers, the typefaces. Those didn't need to change. What was still open was elevation, the rule for how a raised card or a floating menu should look, mostly shadow decisions, and I'd built that once before, on my own, and was never confident it matched everything else.

Instead of patching my old version, I had an AI look at everything that already existed and define elevation from scratch. Then I walked through its answer field by field until it was in place.

Here's the honest part: I've spent the better part of a year, on and off, reading about how design tokens are supposed to work. None of that reading got the last pieces filled in. One afternoon walking through an AI's answers did.

The payoff isn't that one afternoon. It's every afternoon after it. Once a decision has one name and one place it lives, changing "brand blue" later means changing it once, not hunting it down screen by screen.

You don't need to be able to define every rule yourself. You need to know which decisions are yours to make, taste, brand, the calls only you'd make, and which ones you can hand to something built to keep them consistent.
